WSTS — Weighted Schnorr Threshold Signatures

A group of signers, each holding a different number of keys, produces one aggregate signature that verifies as an ordinary Schnorr signature.

The threshold counts keys, not signers

s₁ 4 keys s₂ 1 s₃ 7 s₄ 2 N = 14 T = 10
{s₁, s₃}4 + 7 = 11 ≥ 10 ✓ two signers can sign
{s₁, s₂, s₄}4 + 1 + 2 = 7 < 10 ✗ three cannot

A set of signers meets the threshold when the keys they control sum to at least T. Weight follows stake, so the signing set is not a headcount.

Why it is built this way

One signature, indistinguishable from any other

The aggregate is a standard Schnorr signature and verifies the same way. It costs no more space on chain than a single-signer signature, and linearly less than a traditional multisig.

Optimised for the honest case

The protocol detects misbehaviour and identifies who is responsible, so the common path assumes no byzantine actors and runs in few rounds. Bad actors can then be sanctioned, which is what makes that assumption safe to optimise for.

Weighting is in the construction, not bolted on

Keys are the unit of weight throughout key generation and signing, rather than being emulated by giving one participant several identities.

Two variants

v1

A direct extension of FROST: each signer controls a set of parties, and each party one key. Simple to follow and to audit.

v2

One polynomial and one nonce per party, with a party controlling many keys. Reduces DKG and signing traffic by an order of magnitude at scale.

Before you use it

Read the safety notice. WSTS has been independently audited and ran in production under sBTC, but sBTC vendored version 14.0.0 in March 2026 and the two trees have diverged in both directions since. Neither tree's audit or operational history transfers to the other. Modifying the protocol without a thorough cryptographic review may be insecure.
